# RealTimeX for Businesses

Businesses use RealTimeX when they want AI to stay connected to real processes instead of stopping at one-off prompting.

## What a business gets

- shared workspaces around teams, functions, or customer accounts
- agents that can read company context and act through runtime tools
- governance surfaces for users, credentials, approvals, channels, and logs
- deployment choice across desktop, cloud, and hybrid setups

## Strong fits

RealTimeX is strongest for teams with repeatable operational work, such as:

- **Customer success and account teams:** capture meetings, prep for the next call, generate follow-up, and keep account knowledge close to the workflow.
- **Operations and internal enablement:** turn SOPs, prompts, tools, and runtime actions into governed workflows that teams can actually reuse.
- **Support and service desks:** combine channels, knowledge, agent behavior, and escalation paths instead of relying on isolated bots.
- **Meeting-heavy teams:** keep transcripts, action items, calendar context, and follow-through in the same system.
- **AI rollout programs:** start with one team or operator, then grow into shared workspaces, governance, and broader deployment.

## What businesses can do with it

- connect documents, notes, and knowledge to the teams that use them
- run agent sessions with MCP, browser flows, CLI runtimes, and local apps
- expose approved AI access through channels such as Slack, Telegram, Discord, WhatsApp, and Zalo
- track durable work through goals and Ambient follow-up
- extend the platform with plugins, marketplace items, and provider choice

## A practical rollout path

The best first deployment is usually not "roll out AI to everyone." It is:

1. Pick one team and one visible problem.
2. Create one workspace around that workflow.
3. Load the real documents, notes, or meeting evidence that team already uses.
4. Connect one operational surface such as a channel, a runtime tool, or a local app.
5. Show one repeatable workflow from context to output or action.

After that, expand into more workspaces, more users, more channels, and stronger governance only when the workflow is already proven.

## When RealTimeX may be too much

RealTimeX may be heavier than necessary if:

- you only need single-user prompt chat
- you only want one provider's chat interface with no operational tooling
- you do not need shared workspaces, runtime tools, channels, or deployment flexibility
