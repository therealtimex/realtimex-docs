# Why RealTimeX

RealTimeX is designed for teams that need AI to stay connected to real work, not just answer prompts in isolation.

## The buyer problem

Many AI tools are strong at chat but weak at operations. Teams often end up with:

- one product for chat
- another for documents or retrieval
- another for meetings
- another for automation
- another for runtime tools or approvals

The result is a fragmented stack where the handoff from context to action breaks down.

## What RealTimeX does instead

### It covers the full path from context to execution

RealTimeX brings together:

- workspace chat and retrieval
- documents, notes, and knowledge collections
- meeting capture, calendar context, and follow-up
- agent runtimes, MCP, browser workflows, and local apps
- channels, plugins, and marketplace extensions

### It supports multiple agent runtimes in one governed product

RealTimeX can launch CLI-backed runtimes such as `Claude Code`, `Codex CLI`, `Cursor Agent`, `Antigravity CLI`, `Gemini CLI`, `Qwen Code`, and `OpenCode` from one product surface.

Depending on the runtime, RealTimeX can use the runtime's own login state or inject provider credentials at launch through [Agent Authentication](/agent-authentication). That means teams are not forced into one API-only orchestration pattern for every agent.

### It gives buyers deployment choice

RealTimeX supports:

- **Desktop-first** adoption for fast pilots and local context
- **Cloud** rollout for shared workspaces and centralized administration
- **Hybrid** deployment when some execution or models should stay local

This matters because many teams do not want one forced deployment model from day one.

### It keeps humans in control

RealTimeX is built for operator control, not black-box automation. Teams can manage:

- workspaces and membership
- users and roles
- credentials and stored secrets
- working directories and writable areas
- channel access and pairing
- approvals, monitoring, and logs

See [Agent Runtime](/agent-runtime), [Security](/security), [Users](/users), [Credentials](/credentials), and [Channels](/channels).

## Most AI tools vs RealTimeX

| Most AI tools | RealTimeX |
| --- | --- |
| Strong on chat | Chat, retrieval, meetings, agents, and execution in one system |
| Limited deployment choice | Desktop, cloud, and hybrid adoption paths |
| One narrow provider or runtime story | Mix hosted providers, local models, and multiple agent runtimes |
| Weak operational handoff | Channels, local apps, Ambient follow-up, goals, and runtime tooling |
| Limited operator control | Workspaces, credentials, approvals, directories, logs, and admin surfaces |

## What buyers usually value most

- integration depth across context and action
- deployment flexibility
- operator control and governance
- the ability to start with one workflow and expand without replacing the product
